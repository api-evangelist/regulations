The **Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)** is one of the oldest US financial-data laws, governing how consumer credit information is collected, used, and shared, and giving consumers a right to access and dispute what credit reporting agencies hold about them.

  * **A consumer data-access right, since 1970** - FCRA established, decades before "open banking," that consumers have rights over financial data others hold about them.
  * **Accuracy and dispute** - It requires accuracy and gives consumers a mechanism to correct their records.
  * **Backdrop to §1033** - It is part of the layered US regime the CFPB's Personal Financial Data Rights Rule operates against, extending the access idea from credit files to broader financial data.

FCRA is a reminder that the US did not invent financial-data rights with Section 1033 — it had a partial one for credit data half a century earlier. What §1033 and the CFPB rule add is an *API access* mandate for a consumer's broader financial data, turning a paper-era access right into a machine-readable one — the gap that produced the fragmented US market in my research.
