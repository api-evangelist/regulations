The NAIC is what coordination looks like without compulsion, and its technology estate is a fair reflection
of that position.

  * **Model laws, adopted at state discretion** - The NAIC drafts; each state legislature or department decides.
    Uniformity is an aspiration, not a guarantee.
  * **Accreditation as leverage** - The main lever is accrediting state departments against standards, which
    shapes behaviour indirectly.
  * **SERFF** - Rate and form filing, the highest-volume machine-to-machine transaction in American insurance,
    performed by every carrier in every state.
  * **Statutory reporting** - Annual Statement Blanks, the Market Conduct Annual Statement and the Financial Data
    Repository are the real data flows, and they are regulatory reporting formats rather than APIs.

Two findings from *The State of US Insurance APIs* belong on this entry. SERFF — the filing rail every carrier in
the country depends on — is provisioned by emailing a request address and has no public machine-readable contract.
And a full-text scan of the NAIC's official technology products and services catalog, 1.5 megabytes and 57,226
characters, contains no occurrence of the word *developer*. A regulator's technology catalog that never says
developer is telling you exactly who it believes its audience is.
