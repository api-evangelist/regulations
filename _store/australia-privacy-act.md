The **Privacy Act 1988** is the law that governs personal information in Australia, through the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs). It predates open banking by decades, and the Consumer Data Right was deliberately layered on top of it rather than replacing it.

  * **The Australian Privacy Principles** - Thirteen principles covering collection, use, disclosure, quality, and security of personal information, enforced by the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner.
  * **The CDR's foundation** - The Consumer Data Right adds a sharing *right* and CDR-specific privacy safeguards, but the Privacy Act is the baseline the whole regime respects.
  * **The consent backdrop** - Every CDR consent operates against the Privacy Act's expectations for how personal information is handled.

The Privacy Act is the reason Australia's open-banking consent model looks the way it does — a data-sharing right bolted onto an existing privacy regime. It is a useful counterpart to Europe's GDPR: the same idea that open banking is a consented movement of personal data, and that the movement is only lawful because a privacy law underneath it says how.
