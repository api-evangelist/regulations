**PSD3** and the **Payment Services Regulation (PSR)** are the European Union's attempt to fix what PSD2 got wrong. PSD2 opened the accounts but left execution to national implementations, and the result multiplied into flavors — inconsistent API quality, uneven access, and fraud gaps. PSD3/PSR, still moving through the legislative process, are the response.

  * **A regulation, not just a directive** - Moving core rules into a directly-applicable *regulation* (the PSR) reduces the national-transposition divergence that fragmented PSD2.
  * **Better APIs by requirement** - The package tightens dedicated-interface performance, availability, and access obligations — aiming at the usefulness gap, not just existence.
  * **Part of a wider open-finance push** - It travels alongside the Financial Data Access (FiDA) framework that would extend data sharing beyond payment accounts.

PSD3/PSR is the regulation to watch if you want to know where European open banking is heading. Its whole premise is the lesson at the center of my banking research: a mandate that produces *existence* is not enough; the next iteration has to reach *usefulness*, and that means conformance, performance, and access written into law rather than left to each market.
