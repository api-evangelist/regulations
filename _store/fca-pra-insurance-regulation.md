The UK is the most instructive insurance jurisdiction in this catalog precisely because its regulators did
not cause the outcome the market produced.

  * **FCA — conduct** - Authorisation, conduct of business, consumer duty, and the Financial Services Register.
  * **PRA — prudential** - Capital and solvency supervision under Solvency UK, the post-Brexit successor to
    Solvency II.
  * **Open Finance, still consultation** - The FCA has consulted on extending open-banking-style access to wider
    financial products; there is no open-insurance obligation.
  * **A regulator with a real API** - The FCA's Financial Services Register API is free, self-serve and rate
    limited, and works — one of the few genuinely usable public APIs in UK financial regulation.

*The State of UK Insurance APIs* found the London Market to be the only place in global insurance where a
functioning data standard and real public APIs coexist — reached with no mandate at all, through the coordination
cost of a subscription market that must share a contract document to function. That is the strongest available
evidence that mandates are not the only lever, and it happened in a jurisdiction whose regulators never asked for
it.
