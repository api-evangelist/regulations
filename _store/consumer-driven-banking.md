Canada's **Consumer-Driven Banking framework** is what open banking looks like the moment before it exists. Legislated through the 2024 federal budget and fall economic statement, with the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada named as overseer, it is on the books — and not operational. There is no standard to conform to, no registry, no go-live date in force.

  * **A framework, rebranded** - Canada calls its regime "consumer-driven banking" rather than "open banking," and has legislated the governance, accreditation, and scope in stages.
  * **Legislated, not live** - The law exists; the operating machinery does not yet. A framework that isn't running produces exactly what you'd expect — nothing, yet.
  * **The design question is open** - Whether Canada builds the UK's model (a common spec, conformance, a delivery body) or a minimal read-only obligation the incumbents can satisfy cheaply will decide the entire result.

Canada is my clearest illustration that a mandate on paper is not a mandate in effect. It is the emptiest banking market I have scored — a concentrated Big Six oligopoly with no live rule to force it open and no competitor large enough to substitute for one. The framework is the vacuum's eventual filler; until it goes live, the rails and the bank-owned aggregators hold the surface.
