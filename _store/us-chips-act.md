The **CHIPS and Science Act** is the largest piece of US industrial policy aimed at a single industry
in decades — roughly $52 billion directed at bringing advanced semiconductor manufacturing, packaging
and research back onshore, administered by the CHIPS Program Office at NIST.

  * **Manufacturing incentives** - Direct grants and loan guarantees for fabs, packaging and supplier facilities, plus a manufacturing investment tax credit.
  * **Guardrails** - Recipients accept limits on capacity expansion in countries of concern for a defined period, and constraints on buybacks.
  * **Upside sharing** - Awards above a threshold carry excess-profit-sharing provisions.
  * **R&D programs** - The National Semiconductor Technology Center and advanced packaging programs sit alongside the manufacturing money.

The CHIPS Act is worth cataloguing here for what it demonstrates about where public leverage gets
applied. It is an enormous, highly conditional intervention in this industry, and its conditions are
about **geography, capital allocation and capacity** — where a fab is built and who owns it. Nothing
in it touches what a semiconductor company publishes, how it describes a part, or whether any of its
product data is machine-readable. *The State of Compute & Hardware APIs* scores the silicon layer at
**10.7**, and this is one reason: the most consequential regulation the industry has faced in a
generation never asked.
